EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS – AIR QUALITY

**1. Objective**

To investigate whether cities with more air-quality monitoring stations have higher average pollutant values.

**2. Hypothesis**

Cities with a larger number of monitoring stations may have higher average pollution levels.

**3. Analysis and Evidence**

The dataset was grouped by city. The number of unique monitoring stations and average pollutant values were calculated for each city.

The analysis produced these results:

- Pearson correlation: 0.12
- Spearman correlation: 0.0825
- Cities with fewer stations: 224
- Cities with more stations: 69
- Average pollution in cities with fewer stations: 46.72
- Average pollution in cities with more stations: 49.95

**4. Initial Pattern Investigated**

Some cities with more monitoring stations appeared to have higher pollution values. However, the correlation analysis showed that this pattern was very weak and did not hold consistently across cities.

**5. Final Finding**

The number of monitoring stations has almost no relationship with the average pollutant values in this dataset. More monitoring stations do not necessarily mean higher pollution.

**6. Conclusion**

The analysis shows that monitoring station count alone is not a reliable indicator of pollution levels. Other factors may influence air quality and should be investigated.
